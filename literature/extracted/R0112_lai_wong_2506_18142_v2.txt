                                                  Zahl. The distinction between sticky and non-sticky Kakeya sets plays an important

                                                  to resolve the conjecture. In this paper, we explicitly construct a non-sticky Kakeya

                                                  non-trivial sticky and non-sticky Kakeya sets in high dimension that are not formed

                                                                                 1. Introduction

                                              A Kakeya set (or a Bescovitch set) in Rd is a compact set containing a unit line
                                           segment in every direction. The Kakeya set conjecture states that every Kakeya set
                                           has Hausdorff dimension d. This conjecture is one of the most notoriously difficult,
                                           and significant, conjectures in harmonic analysis and geometric measure theory, and
                                           it is implied by several other important conjectures, such as the Fourier restriction
                                           conjecture and Bochner-Riesz conjecture in harmonic analysis. For more background,
                                           readers can consult the books [10, 17].
                                              The conjecture can be proved fairly easily in R2 , which Davies did in the 1970s [4]
                                           (see also [10]). Recently, the conjecture was resolved in R3 by Wang and Zahl via a
                                           series of seminal works [14, 15, 16]. One of the key ingredients in their proof was to
                                           distinguish two classes of Kakeya sets known as the sticky and the non-sticky Kakeya
                                           sets. The stickiness property was first formulated by Katz, Laba, and Tao in early
                                           2000 [8]. In 2014, Tao laid down a strategy to attack the Kakeya conjecture in his
                                           blog [12]; in summary, if a hypothetical counterexample to the Kakeya set conjecture
                                           existed, the set ought to be planey, grainy, and sticky. While planiness and graininess
                                           were well-characterized in [2] and [7] (see also [9]), the correct formulation of stickiness
                                           was not clear at that time. Wang and Zahl advanced Tao’s approach by defining an
                                           explicit notion of stickiness and showed first that a sticky Kakeya set in R3 must be
                                           of full Hausdorff dimension [14], before settling the conjecture on R3 in full generality
                                           [16]. In [14], they also proposed the

                                           Sticky Kakeya set conjecture: All sticky Kakeya sets in Rd must have Hausdorff
                                           dimension d.

Even in this restricted version, the conjecture is open for d ≥ 4, with some partial
results for R4 in [3].
  To describe stickiness precisely, we will need to define a space of affine lines.
An affine line on Rd , not lying in the hyperplane {(x1 , · · · , xd ) : xd = 0}, can be
parametrized by
(1.1)                      Lv,a (t) = (a, 0) + t(v, 1), t ∈ R,
where a, v ∈ Rd−1 . Let A be the collection of all such lines. Technically, we are
missing the lines that lie on the hyperplane xd = 0. However, the missing directions
only form a lower-dimensional set in the space of directions, so there is no loss of
generality to disregard this set of lines. We now endow A with a metric on R2d−2 by
                         d(Lv,a , Lw,b ) = ∥a − b∥ + ∥v − w∥.
Suppose that K is a Kakeya set in Rd . Then for all v ∈ Rd−1 , there exists av such
that Lv,av ∩ K contains a unit line segment. Let
              AK = {(v, av ) : Lv,av ∩ K contains a unit line segment}.
Because the set has full dimension in v by the definition of Kakeya set, the Hausdorff
dimension of AK in R2d−2 is at least d − 1.
Definition 1.1. We say that a Kakeya set K is a sticky Kakeya set if the packing
dimension of AK is equal to d − 1. Otherwise, K is a non-sticky Kakeya set (i.e.,
the packing dimension of AK is strictly larger than d − 1).

   As a simple example, the unit disk is a non-sticky Kakeya set, since each direction
corresponds to an interval of initial points, making the set AK dimension 2, and indeed
of positive Lebesgue measure. On the other hand, the classic example of a measure
zero Kakeya set is the one based on the four-corner self-similar Cantor set K, which
has Hausdorff dimension 1. This construction can be found in [11, Chapter 7] (see
also Section 4). In this construction, AK = K, which also has packing dimension
equal to 1, hence K is a sticky Kakeya set.
   Although a restriction to Kakeya sets of Lebesgue measure zero was not used in
the proof of the Kakeya conjecture in R3 , it is clear that we can, without loss of
generality, assume the set has measure zero (since sets with positive measures have
full Hausdorff dimension). As there appear to be no known examples of a non-sticky
Kakeya set of Lebesgue measure zero, one might conjecture that non-sticky Kakeya
sets must have positive Lebesgue measure, so that the sticky Kakeya set conjecture
and the original Kakeya set conjecture are equivalent. In this paper, we show that
this is not the case; the main result of this paper is
Theorem 1.2. There exists a non-sticky Kakeya set of Lebesgue measure zero in R2 ,
and hence in Rd , for all d > 2.

  A construction for Rd with d > 2 can be made by simply taking the Cartesian
product of a two-dimensional Kakeya set with Rd−2 . In the literature, apart from this
