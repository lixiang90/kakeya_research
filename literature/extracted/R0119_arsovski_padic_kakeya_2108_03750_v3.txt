                                          THE P -ADIC KAKEYA CONJECTURE
arXiv:2108.03750v3 [math.NT] 1 Nov 2021




                                                                      BODAN ARSOVSKI




                                                 Abstract. We prove that all bounded subsets of Qnp containing
                                                 a line segment of unit length in every direction have Hausdorff
                                                 and Minkowski dimension n. This is the analogue of the classical
                                                 Kakeya conjecture with R replaced by Qp .

                                                                      1. Introduction
                                             In 1917, Kakeya posed the Kakeya needle problem, asking about
                                          the minimum area of a region in the plane in which a needle of unit
                                          length can be rotated around by 360◦ . The problem reduces to asking
                                          about the minimum area of a Kakeya set in R2 —that is, a bounded
                                          subset of R2 containing a line segment of unit length in every direction.
                                          Besicovitch [Bes63] showed that, in a certain sense, the answer is “arbi-
                                          trarily small”, by constructing a Kakeya set in R2 of Lebesgue measure
                                          zero. On the other hand, Davies [Dav71] showed that, in a different
                                          sense, the answer is “large”: a Kakeya set in R2 must have Minkowski
                                          dimension 2. The construction of [Bes63] extends immediately to Rn
                                          with n > 3. The higher-dimensional analogue of the result of [Dav71]
                                          is much more difficult: it is the Minkowski dimension version of the
                                          notorious Kakeya conjecture, which is one of the most important open
                                          problems in geometric measure theory, and analysis in general.
                                          Conjecture A (Kakeya). Let n be a positive integer. All Kakeya sets
                                          in Rn have Hausdorff and Minkowski dimension n.
                                             The Kakeya conjecture has deep connections with harmonic anal-
                                          ysis, PDEs, and combinatorics among other fields, and it is open for
                                          n > 3. For n ∈ {3, 4}, the state of the art are the results of Katz–
                                          Zahl [KZ19, KZ21] that all Kakeya sets in R3 have Hausdorff and
                                          Minkowski dimension at least 5/2 + ε (for some ε > 0), and all Kakeya
                                          sets in R4 have Hausdorff and Minkowski dimension at least 3.059 (see
                                          also Wolff [Wol95], Katz–Łaba–Tao [KŁT00], and Łaba–Tao [ŁT01] for
                                          previous results). For n > 5, the state of the art is the result of Katz–
                                          Tao [KT02] that all Kakeya sets in Rn have Hausdorff dimension at least

   As a possible approach to the Kakeya conjecture, Wolff [Wol99] sug-
gested an analogous conjecture over finite fields, which was then proved
by Dvir [Dvi09]. As noted by Ellenberg–Oberlin–Tao [EOT10], the
analogy between the classical and finite field Kakeya conjectures is
imperfect for two reasons. Firstly, there is no natural non-discrete no-
tion of distance in finite vector spaces, and the discrete distance does
not have multiple scales. By contrast, the Euclidean distance in Rn has
multiple scales. Secondly, the result of [Dvi09] is too strong: all Kakeya
sets in Fnq (where Fq is a finite field) have, in a certain sense, positive
measure. In particular, there is no variant in Fnq of the construction of
Besicovitch of a Kakeya set of measure zero.
   In light of these two shortcomings, Ellenberg–Oberlin–Tao [EOT10]
suggested analogues of the Kakeya conjecture over topological fields
whose metrics capture the multiple scales property of the Euclidean
metric of Rn . The only (non-discrete) locally compact Hausdorff topo-
logical fields are the finite extensions of R, Qp , and Fq ((t)) (for vary-
ing primes p and prime powers q). Moreover, the Kakeya conjecture
over K ∈ {R, Qp , Fq ((t))} implies the Kakeya conjecture over any fi-
nite extension L/K, simply by treating Ln as a vector space over K.
Therefore, other than the classical setting over R, the two other nat-
ural settings for the Kakeya conjecture (with interesting metrics that
have multiple scales) are over Qp and Fq ((t)). Both shortcomings are
resolved in these settings. Firstly, Qnp and Fq ((t))n have metrics that
have multiple scales, just like the metric of Rn . Secondly, there are
variants of the Besicovitch construction in both settings: Dummit–
Hablicsek [DH11] constructed a Kakeya set in Fq ((t))n of measure zero,
Hickman–Wright [HW18] constructed a Kakeya set in Qnp of measure
zero, and Fraser [Fra16] constructed more generally a Kakeya set in
Ln of measure zero, for any finite extension L/K of K ∈ {Qp , Fq ((t))}.
Moreover, Dummit–Hablicsek [DH11] proved the analogues of the re-
sult of Davies [Dav71] in Q2p and Fq ((t))2 . All of these theorems suggest
that the Kakeya conjectures over Qp and Fq ((t)) could be good models
for the classical Kakeya conjecture over R.
   In this article we prove the Kakeya conjecture over Qp :

Theorem 1. Let p be a prime number and n a positive integer. All
Kakeya sets in Qnp have Hausdorff and Minkowski dimension n.

Remark 2. Let p and n be as in theorem 1, and let K/Qp be a finite
extension. Theorem 1 then implies that all Kakeya sets in K n have

Hausdorff and Minkowski dimension n, i.e. the Kakeya conjecture over
all p-adic local fields (simply by treating K n as a vector space over Qp ).
Remark 3. The field of p-adic numbers Qp is the completion of Q with
respect to the p-adic norm |x|p = p−vp (x) , and, in fact, the only com-
pletions of Q are R and Qp (for varying p). To give some intuition
behind its topology, the closed unit ball Zp of Qp is homeomorphic to
the Cantor set C ⊆ [0, 1]. There is a well developed analogue of classi-
cal analysis over the p-adic numbers called p-adic analysis, which has
historically been of particular interest in number theory.
   The proof of theorem 1 is inspired by Dvir’s famous proof of the finite
field Kakeya conjecture [Dvi09], and the method can be summarized
as “the polynomial method over discrete valuation rings”. This dis-
crete valuation polynomial method seems flexible and extensible, and
we hope that it can be used to adapt other theorems with known poly-
nomial method proofs, from the finite field setting over Fq , to the p-adic
setting over K/Qp (and in particular over the finite rings Z/pk Z). We
prioritize brevity and clarity of exposition, so the implied constants are
not sharp and can be improved by more careful analysis1.

Definition 4. Let ε, δ ∈ [0, 1]. Let us normalize the measures of the
