                                                   A NEW BOUND FOR THE GAUSS CIRCLE PROBLEM

                                                         XI CEN, XITAO GAO, CHENG ZHANG, AND JUNYONG ZHANG


                                                  Abstract. Let E(X) = #{(a, b) ∈ Z2 : a2 + b2 ≤ X} − πX. We prove that
                                                  E(X) = Oε (X 209/670+ε ) for every ε > 0, uniformly for real X ≥ 4. This breaks
arXiv:2610.21730v1 [math.NT] 15 Oct 2026




                                                  the 5/16 threshold suggested by Huxley as a possible limit of the Bombieri–Iwaniec
                                                  method at the time. The proof introduces two refinements: a cone distribution
                                                  estimate that preserves separate fourth- and sixth-power contributions in the double
                                                  large sieve, and an explicit inversion of Taylor-coefficient constraints that cancels
                                                  the linear error in a pair-counting argument. These refinements, together with the
                                                  Robert–Sargos and Watt spacing estimates and the Vinogradov mean value theorem,
                                                  yield the improvement.

                                           1.1. Main result. For real X ≥ 0, define the circle remainder by

   Several advances arose first in the divisor problem. Kolesnik obtained the exponents
12/37, 346/1067 and 35/108 in 1969, 1973 and 1982, respectively [27–29]. Nowak
[43] transferred the last of these to the circle problem through divisors in arithmetic
progressions. Kolesnik’s later exponent-pair refinement gave 139/429 [30].
   The Bombieri–Iwaniec method [2], adapted to the circle and divisor problems by
Iwaniec–Mozzochi [25], introduced a new approach based on rational approximation
and the double large sieve. It reduces the exponential sums to first- and second-spacing
problems. Huxley’s development of this method [19–22] culminated in [22, 24]

cones. Li–Yang [39] then combined Guth–Maldague’s small-cap decoupling theorem [11]
with second-spacing estimates to improve Huxley’s exponent.
