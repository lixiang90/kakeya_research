                                                                                           Abstract
                                                    We obtain a slight improvement to the trilinear bound of Guth-Zahl by application of Wang-
                                                 Zahl’s resolution of 3-D Kakeya to tubes inside a thickened neighborhood of a variety. This is
                                                 combined with the planebrush argument of Katz and Zahl to obtain the new bound of 3.073.


                                           1     Introduction
                                           A Besicovitch set (also called a Kakeya set) in Rn is a compact subset of Rn containing a unit line
                                           segment in every direction. The Kakeya conjecture asserts that every Besicovitch set in Rn must
                                           have Hausdorff dimension n. For n = 2, the conjecture was proved by Davies in 1971 [4]. The
                                           3-dimensional case was proved by Wang and Zahl [13, 14, 15] in a major recent breakthrough. The
                                           conjecture remains open for n ≥ 4, though partial results exist. See [20] for a recent survey of
                                           progress on the Kakeya conjecture by Zahl.
                                               In this paper, we make some partial progress towards the Kakeya conjecture in R4 . Specifically,
                                           we will prove the following result.

                                                Let us briefly discuss the recent history of progress on the Kakeya conjecture in R4 . In 2018,
                                           Guth and Zahl [5] (in conjunction with the results of Zahl [18], and independently Katz-Rogers [7])
                                           proved that Kakeya sets in R4 have Hausdorff dimension at least 3.025. The primary ingredients
                                           used are the polynomial Wolff axioms and grains decomposition, which were used to obtain a new
                                           trilinear Kakeya estimate.
                                                Following this, Katz and Zahl [8] improved the Hausdorff dimension bound to 3.059 using the
                                           planebrush argument. This was a higher dimensional analogue of Wolff’s hairbrush argument [16].
                                           This in fact yields a Hausdorff dimension bound of 3, which was the longstanding record before the
                                           bound of Guth and Zahl [5]. Katz and Zahl [8] also used the trilinear bound of Guth-Zahl in their
                                           induction. A finite field version of the planebrush argument can be found in the expository paper
                                           [9].
                                                For our result, we will be obtaining a slight improvement to the trilinear bound of Guth-Zahl
                                           [5] by application of Wang-Zahl’s resolution of 3-D Kakeya [15]. This is then plugged in to the
                                           induction of Katz and Zahl [8] to obtain the new bound of 3.073.
                                                We will be working in the ambient space R4 throughout this paper.

4     Applying Wang-Zahl inside a polynomial grain
In this section, we will be working with tubes which are contained in the δ-neighborhood of a low
degree variety in R4 (also called a grain, see Definition 24). After various technical manipulations,
we will apply the 3-dimensional Wang-Zahl result [15] to this configuration of tubes. This is the
key new ingredient in the proof, eventually yielding the improved dimension bound.

4.1    Preliminaries
A set of tubes is said to satisfy the Wolff axioms if not too many tubes from the set can concentrate
into the thickened neighborhood of an affine subspace. More precisely, we have the following.
Definition 13. Let T be a collection of δ-tubes. We say that it satisfies the linear Wolff axioms if at
most Ct1 t2 . . . tn−1 δ 1−n tubes are contained in a rectangular prism of dimensions 1×t1 ×t2 ×. . . tn−1 .
    A set of tubes is said to satisfy the polynomial Wolff axioms if not too many tubes from the
set can concentrate into the thickened neighborhood of an algebraic variety, or more generally a
semi-algebraic set. This will play a crucial role in reaching our objective.
Definition 14. Let T be a collection of δ-tubes. We say that it satisfies the polynomial Wolff
axioms if for every semialgebraic set S ⊂ Rn of complexity at most E, and every δ ≤ λ ≤ 1,

                             |{T ∈ T : |T ∩ S| ≥ λ|T |}| ≤ KE |S|δ 1−n λ−n .                            (8)

In this section, we shall state the results from the paper of Katz and Zahl [8] that we will be using.
Proofs will be omitted for the sake of brevity and can be found in the aforementioned reference.
First we need the following definition.

Definition 28 (Planyness). Let (T, Y ) be a set of δ-tubes and their associated shading. We say
that (T, Y ) is plany if for each δ-cube Q, there is a plane Π(Q) so that for all T ∈ TY (Q) we have
∠(v(T ), Π(Q)) ≲ δ.

   Next we need the following technical lemma which states that tubes are either plany or trilinear.
This is Lemma 33 from Katz-Zahl [8].

(ϵ1 , C1 )-two-ends and (ϵ2 , C2 )-robustly transverse.
