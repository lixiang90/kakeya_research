                                                                                     1. Introduction
                                              In 1975, Szemerédi [20] proved that subsets of [n] that have no k-term arithmetic progres-
                                           sions have size at most o(n). Since then, there have been many important generalisations of
                                           this result. Particularly relevant here is the work of Bergelson and Leibman [6], who estab-
                                           lished that the same holds if we require the subsets of [n] to avoid only k-APs whose common
                                           difference is a perfect ℓ-th power, for some fixed ℓ ∈ N. The set of common differences in this
                                           case is very sparse, but also very structured, so a natural question is how sparse a typical
                                           set of common differences can be while retaining that property.
                                              One formulation of this problem, known as Szemerédi’s theorem with random differences,
                                           is the following. Determine the threshold p = p(n) such that, if D is a p-random subset
                                           of [n], then with high probability every subset of [n] that avoids k-APs with common dif-
                                           ference in D has size o(n). The first non-trivial bounds for this problem were established
                                           independently by Christ [13] and Frantzikinakis, Lesigne and Wierdl [15], who proved that
                                                                            k−2
                                           this holds whenever p ≥ n−1/2 +o(1) . This was later improved for k ≥ 5 to p ≥ n−1/k+o(1)
                                           by Frantzikinakis, Lesigne and Wierdl [16]. The best current bound is that p ≥ n−2/k+o(1)
                                           suffices, first established by Briët, Dvir and Gopi [9] when k is even (see also [10]) and then
                                           by Briët and Castro-Silva [8] when it is odd.
                                              Frantzikinakis, Lesigne and Wierdl [16] conjectured, however, that these bounds are still
                                           far from the truth: they believe that pn/ log n → ∞ should suffice for fixed k. Altman [4]
                                           moreover proved that if their conjecture is true, then it depends on the structure of the
                                           integers: we now know (see also [7, 5]) that the analogous question in the setting of Fdq for
                                           a fixed prime q ≥ k requires p ≳ (log n)k−1 /n, where n = q d .
                                              Our main result is an improvement over the p ≥ n−2/k+o(1) bound for all sufficiently large
                                           k. To state it, let [n]p denote a p-random subset of [n] and

   While the strategies of both Briët, Dvir and Gopi and Briët and Castro-Silva are tech-
nical, relying on connections to coding theory, our approach is more elementary. The main
inspiration for our work comes from the problem of determining the independence number
of sparse random Cayley sum graphs [11, 19, 3], which is closely related to the (non-trivial)
case of Szemerédi’s theorem with random differences for k = 2.
   A common feature of the strategies in [11, 19, 3] (inspired by previous work of Alon [1, 2])
is to replace a union bound over all candidate independent sets by one over a much smaller
family. In the formulation of Alon and Pham [3], this family is a small cover of sumsets:
for every large set A, its sumset A + A contains a set in the cover. The sets in the cover
are moreover required to be sufficiently large. These two properties, combined with the fact
that the family is small, imply that taking a union bound over the cover succeeds, whereas
a naive union bound over all independent sets fails.
   To understand the connection between the approach in the Cayley context and the current
problem, let Hk be the hypergraph whose vertex set is [n] and each hyperedge corresponds to
a non-trivial k-AP. Further let Hk,p be the random hypergraph obtained by keeping only the
edges of Hk that have common difference in [n]p . In this setting1, Theorem 1.1 is bounding
the probability that α(Hk,p ) ≥ δn.
   We similarly deduce Theorem 1.1 from a small cover lemma, Lemma 1.2 below. The main
difference in our argument is that instead of each set F in the cover being sufficiently large,
it will have large Dk (F ).
Lemma 1.2. There exists c > 0 such that the following holds for every k ≥ 3 and δ > 0. Let
C = C(k) > 0 and γ = γ(δ, k) > 0, and let n be sufficiently large. There exists a collection

                                   2. Proof of Lemma 1.2
  In the proof of Lemma 1.2, we will use two results. The first is a standard result of
Varnavides [21], a “supersaturation” form of Szemerédi’s theorem.
Theorem 2.1 ([21], see [22, Theorem 9.3.1]). Let k ∈ N, δ > 0 and let n ∈ N be sufficiently
large. If S ⊂ [n] satisfies |S| ≥ δn, then it contains at least εn2 k-APs, for ε = ε(δ, k) > 0.
   The other, and crucial, input is the following result of Green and Ruzsa [18], which they
relate to the arithmetic Kakeya conjecture of Katz and Tao.
Theorem 2.2 ([18, Theorem 1.7]). There exists c > 0 such that the following holds for every
k ≥ 3 for some C = C(k) > 0. For every sufficiently large n ∈ N, there exists Y ⊂ [Cn]
with |Y | ≤ n1−c/ log log k which contains, for every d ∈ [n], a k-AP with common difference d.
   The construction of Y takes a single page of Green and Ruzsa’s paper [18, Section 5].
They first construct a smaller set Y ′ ⊂ [Ck ], selecting the starting point of each k-AP to be
the square of its common difference modulo Q, the product of the first few odd primes. The
Chinese remainder theorem allows them to bound the size of the set of the j-th terms of all
the k-APs for each j ∈ [k], and then of the entire Y ′ by Q1−c/ log log k . They finally obtain Y
from Y ′ using the base Q representations of the common differences d ∈ [n].
   As will become clear in the proof of Lemma 1.2 below, improving the upper bound on |Y |
in Theorem 2.2 would give a corresponding improvement for the threshold in Szemerédi’s
theorem with random differences. This reduction then connects two problems in Green’s list
of 100 open problems, which also highlighted the case k = 3 of Szemerédi’s theorem with
random differences [17, Problems 10 and 37].
   However, it is worth noting that no improvement in Theorem 2.2 would lead to a poly-
nomial improvement for the known bounds when k ∈ {3, 4}. To see why, observe that the
case k = 2 is equivalent to [n] ⊂ Y − Y , and that forces |Y | ≥ n1/2 . On the other hand, the
results of Briët and Castro-Silva (in fact, even those in [9, 10] for k = 4) already imply that
the threshold in these cases is at most n−1/2+o(1) .
