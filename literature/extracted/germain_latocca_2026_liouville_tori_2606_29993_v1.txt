                                                  STRICHARTZ ESTIMATES FOR THE LIOUVILLE EQUATION ON
                                                      EUCLIDEAN TORI AND APPLICATIONS TO KAKEYA

                                                                               PIERRE GERMAIN & MICKAËL LATOCCA


                                                   Abstract. We prove Strichartz estimates for the space-time density ρ of solutions to the free
                                                   Liouville equation on flat tori. In dimension one, we obtain the optimal range of estimates for
                                                   the density ρ ∈ Lpt,x in terms of f0 ∈ Lav Lbx . In higher dimensions, we prove that such estimates
                                                   cannot hold and that a weight has to be added: ρ can be bounded in terms of the norm of |v|γ f0 .

                                                   We conjecture a range of optimal estimates, and partially prove them. Finally, these results have
                                                   natural applications to the X-ray transform and Kakeya problems on Euclidean cylinders.

1.4. Related directions.

1.4.1. Strichartz estimates for the Schrödinger equation. This is where it all started! After
Strichartz’ original paper [Str77] regarding estimates in the whole space, Bourgain [Bou93]
initiated the study of estimates on the torus of the type

                                    ∥eit∆ f0 ∥Lpt,x ([0,1]×Td ) ≲ ∥f0 ∥H s (Td ) ,

(where p ∈ [1, ∞], s ≥ 0) which was completed in [BD15]. One can also ask for estimates of the
type
                                     ∥eit∆ f0 ∥Lpt,x ([0,1]×Td ) ≲ ∥fb0 ∥Lq (Td )

(where p, q ∈ [1, ∞]) but finding the optimal range of (p, q) is an open problem, equivalent to the
Fourier restriction problem.
   The estimates above on eit∆ f0 and (2), (3) have common features: the left-hand side of
the equation is a space-time Lp norm, while the right-hand side norm is invariant through
the flow of the linear problem (Liouville of Schrödinger). Furthermore, the Schrödinger and
Liouville equations on the torus are intimately tied, since they are exchanged by the Wigner
transform—though it does not seem possible to use this correspondence to derive analytical
estimates.
