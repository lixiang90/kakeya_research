                                                    A NOTE ON KAKEYA SETS OF HORIZONTAL AND SLp2q LINES

                                                                         KATRIN FÄSSLER AND TUOMAS ORPONEN


                                                    A BSTRACT. We consider unions of SLp2q lines in R3 . These are lines of the form
arXiv:2210.09955v1 [math.CA] 18 Oct 2022




                                                                                    L “ pa, b, 0q ` spanpc, d, 1q,
                                                    where ad ´ bc “ 1. We show that if L is a Kakeya set of SLp2q lines, then the union YL
                                                    has Hausdorff dimension 3. This answers a question of Wang and Zahl.
                                                        The SLp2q lines can be identified with horizontal lines in the first Heisenberg group, and
                                                    we obtain the main result as a corollary of a more general statement concerning unions of
                                                    horizontal lines. This statement is established via a point-line duality principle between
                                                    horizontal and conical lines in R3 , combined with recent work on restricted families of pro-
                                                    jections to planes, due to Gan, Guo, Guth, Harris, Maldague, and Wang.
                                                        Our result also has a corollary for Nikodym sets associated with horizontal lines, which
                                                    answers a special case of a question of Kim.

1.1. Nikodym sets associated with horizontal lines. Theorem 1.5 easily yields informa-
tion about the dimension of Nikodym sets associated with horizontal lines. A set N Ă R3
is called an LpHq-Nikodym set if for every p P R3 (or more generally every p P R3 in a
measurable set of positive measure Ω Ă R3 ) there exists a line ℓp P LpHq containing p
such that N contains a line segment Ip Ă ℓp of positive length.

repeat the standard details below for the reader’s convenience. For a similar argument
in the case of classical Kakeya and Nikodym sets, see [8, Section 11.3].

1.2. Ingredients of the proof. The proof of Theorem 1.5 is based on two ingredients.
The first one is a point-line duality between horizontal lines and conical lines in R3 , namely
translates of lines contained in the light cone tpx, y, tq : t2 “ x2 ` y 2 u. This duality was
formalised in our paper [2], although it was already implicit in the work [6] of Liu. Using
this point-line duality, Kakeya-type problems for horizontal lines can be transformed
into projection problems in R3 . These projection problems concern "restricted" families
of projections to planes in R3 . Sharp results for such families were recently established
by Gan, Guo, Guth, Harris, Maldague, and Wang [3]. This is the second key component
in the proof of Theorem 1.5.
