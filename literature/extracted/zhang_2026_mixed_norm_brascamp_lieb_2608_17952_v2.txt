                                                MIXED-NORM BRASCAMP-LIEB INEQUALITIES

                                                                            RUIXIANG ZHANG
arXiv:2608.17952v2 [math.CA] 19 Aug 2026




                                                  Abstract. Christ [Chr01] looked at a certain boundedness problem
                                                  for trilinear operators. In this paper we set up a framework of mixed-
                                                  norm Brascamp-Lieb inequalities and identify a new interesting regime
                                                  not covered by the study of classical Brascamp-Lieb. Positive results in
                                                  [Chr01] can be viewed as the first nontrivial progress in this new regime.
                                                  We will also prove another mixed-norm Brascamp-Lieb inequality, show-
                                                  casing how one can use a tensor product trick to slightly sharpen Christ’s
                                                  argument and also obtain the endpoint case. We then discuss the connec-
                                                  tions between mixed-norm Brascamp-Lieb and the Kakeya Conjectures,
                                                  as well as unique new difficulties for these mixed-norm Brascamp-Lieb
                                                  inequalities compared to the classical setting.

Kakeya Conjectures [Bou99, KT99, KT02b]. Along this line we explain a
natural connection (Theorem 5.3) between (5) and Kakeya: If one can take
q arbitrarily close to 1, then the Hausdorff version of Kakeya (in any dimen-

(see also [KT99, KT02b]) but is only possible because of very recent progress
on quantitative Szemerédi’s Theorem [GT09, GT17, LSS24a, LSS24b]. The
literature only seems to have the Minkowski dimension version2, so we work
out necessary modifications for the Hausdorff version.
   We will also turn to a technical comparison between (5) and (2). When
q < 2 there are key differences between natures of the two. As Christ [Chr01]
pointed out, unlike (2), the extremizers for the q < 2 case of (5) are in general
not symmetric non-decreasing. We will show another difference based on
the techniques in [Chr01]: A natural and desirable perturbed version of
(5) can never hold. Thus, new challenges show up if one wants to apply
recent developments like [BCT06, Gut10, Gut15, BBFL18] in the mixed-
norm setting for q < 2.

1.5. Outline of the paper. In Section 2, we formulate the perturbed
Brascamp-Lieb inequality and present some basic knowledge about them. In
Section 3, we prove that knowing the restricted weak type estimates in the
discrete setting automatically implies the endpoint mixed-norm Brascamp-
Lieb. In Section 4, we prove (1) as an application of the above principle.
We discuss connections to the Kakeya Conjectures and absence of the per-
turbed version in Section 5 and leave a technical but standard argument to
Appendix A.

5. Connecting mixed-norm Brascamp-Lieb in R2 to Kakeya in Rd
   The inequality (5) has connections to the Kakeya Conjectures. We explain
this connection and make an interesting remark about a failed attempt to
apply recent progress on Brascamp-Lieb to (5) in this section. We first
introduce the Kakeya Conjectures.
                  MIXED-NORM BRASCAMP-LIEB INEQUALITIES                             11

5.1. Brief introduction to Kakeya. On a high level, the Kakeya Conjec-
tures assert the tubes in different directions in Rd cannot overlap unexpect-
edly much. Different forms of those conjectures then have different ways to
quantify this. We introduce one below.
Definition 5.1 (Kakeya set). A compact set E ⊂ Rd is called a Kakeya set
if E contains a unit line segment in every direction.
Conjecture 5.2 (Kakeya Conjecture, Hausdorff version). Every Kakeya set
in Rd has Hausdorff dimension d.
   Kakeya Conjectures remain open in dimensions d ≥ 4. They are dif-
ficult geometric measure theory problems themselves, but are surprisingly
important also in modern Fourier analysis. For a good introduction of the
conjectures and the above connection, see [Tao01]. For some recent progress
on the Kakeya Conjectures, see e.g. [KZ19, Zah21, HRZ22, Ars24] and the
notable resolution of Conjecture 5.2 in dimension 3 [WZ26, WZ25a, WZ25b].

5.2. The connection to (5). There is a natural connection between the
mixed-norm Brascamp-Lieb inequality (5) and the Kakeya Conjectures in
the q < 2 regime. Before carefully explaining it, let us remark that the
arithmetic projection technique in [KT99] that we used to prove (1) was
a further development following Bourgain’s work [Bou99] to use additive
combinatorics to improve on the high-dimensional Kakeya Conjectures. This
technique was developed further later in [KT02b]. It is conceivable that more
results in [KT99, KT02b] can be upgraded to prove new cases of (5), just
like what we did to prove (1).
   We do not try to prove more cases of (5) in this paper. We chose to present
and to prove the current Theorem 1.1 here just because it is technically
easiest. Instead, we turn to a slightly different perspective: There is an
abstract connection between (5) for q < 2 and Conjecture 5.2 (in arbitrary
dimension d). We now state and prove this connection.
